Privacy Policy

Application: Chaquant Protocol (Package: tech.chaquant.protocol) • Effective Date: October 2026
Core Commitment: Chaquant Protocol operates under a strict Zero-Data Collection Architecture. We do not require accounts, phone numbers, email addresses, contact access, or advertising identifiers. All cryptographic operations occur exclusively in-memory on your physical device.

1. Data We Do NOT Collect

By design, our protocol eliminates metadata leakage and central surveillance. Chaquant does NOT collect, store, transmit, or share:

  • No Personal Identifiers: No names, email addresses, phone numbers, or account usernames.
  • No Contact Information: We never request access to your device address book or contact lists.
  • No Location Data: No GPS, Wi-Fi, or cellular network location tracking.
  • No Advertising Identifiers: We explicitly opt-out of Google Advertising ID (AD_ID) and Apple IDFA.
  • No Usage Analytics or Crash Trackers: No third-party SDKs (Google Firebase, Facebook SDK, Mixpanel, etc.).

2. Local On-Device Cryptographic Vault

All transient messages, cryptographic keys, and document representations are stored exclusively on your device using hardware-backed encryption:

  • SQLCipher (256-bit AES-GCM): Local database encrypted with high-entropy keys derived on-device.
  • Hardware Security Module: Key material is rooted in Android Keystore / Apple Secure Enclave.
  • Zero Cloud Backups: System backups (Google Drive / iCloud Keychain) are explicitly disabled (allowBackup="false") to prevent unauthorized extraction.

3. Post-Quantum Encryption & Transport Obfuscation

Communications between channel participants leverage state-of-the-art cryptographic standards:

  • NIST Level-5 Post-Quantum Cryptography: FIPS 203 (ML-KEM-1024) for quantum-resistant key exchange and FIPS 204 (ML-DSA-87) for digital signatures.
  • Uniform Sphinx Framing: Packets are padded to uniform 1024-byte frames to prevent traffic analysis.
  • Blind Silent Push: Background wakeups contain zero plaintext and zero metadata payload (content-available: 1).

4. Irreversible Data Destruction (GDPR Article 17 Compliance)

Chaquant solves the regulatory Right-to-be-Forgotten trilemma:

  • Physical Crypto-Shredding: When an ephemeral channel is closed or its Time-to-Live (TTL) expires, the underlying encryption keys are permanently deleted from device hardware, rendering any NAND flash residual data mathematically undecryptable.
  • Aztec L2 Siloed Nullifiers: A deterministic nullifier is emitted to Aztec Network's global Nullifier Tree. This generates a publicly auditable Cryptographic Destruction Receipt that proves channel deletion to legal and compliance auditors without disclosing participant identities or contents.

5. Minimal Device Permissions

Chaquant declares only the minimum operating system permissions strictly required for sovereign execution:

  • CAMERA: Used exclusively to scan out-of-band post-quantum invitation QR codes. Camera frames are processed strictly in RAM and never saved or transmitted.
  • USE_BIOMETRIC: Used to authorize local hardware enclave decryption via TouchID / FaceID / Fingerprint.
  • INTERNET: Required to route encrypted Sphinx packets to the decentralized network.
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS / RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED: Required for the zero-payload Blind Silent Push channel.
